  1. Drive the robot slowly with teleop.
  2. Cartographer builds the map from LiDAR only.
  3. A small node corrects the scan timing first. Without it, Cartographer dropped part of each scan.
  4. Return on the same path. This gives a clean loop closure.
  5. Save the map as a .pgm file for AMCL.
Why LiDAR only?

Our wheel odometry was not reliable at that time. Bad odometry bent the map.

Stopping on the centre

08 · Precision approach
1

Nav2 drives the robot to within 10 cm of the cell centre.

2

The script makes small moves: Spin, DriveOnHeading, BackUp (max 3 cm). Nav2 checks each move for collisions.

3

The robot stays still for 1 s.

4

Five new AMCL poses must be within 2 cm of the target and within 1 cm of each other.

5

Wait 3 s, then go to the next goal.

Why not Nav2 only?

Near the goal, Nav2 sends low-speed commands. At low speed the wheels slip. Odometry then says the robot is at the goal, but it is not: in run 2 it was 4.8–6.2 cm off. So we trust AMCL, not odometry. AMCL matches the LiDAR scan to the map.

If the fine approach fails

The robot stops, announces the missed cell and goes on.

This is our own custom XML tree, no_global_clear.xml. bt_navigator loads it in place of the Nav2 default.

Keep the global costmap

The default recovery clears both costmaps. Then the robot forgets the obstacle and picks the same blocked path again. Our recovery clears only the local costmap.

Spin recovery is off

The default tree had a Spin recovery. It sometimes turned the wrong way. We use back up only. The Rotation Shim then turns the robot to face the new path.

Challenges on the robot

12 · Problem → fix → result
01

A 10 cm error in every scan

ProblemThe URDF had the LiDAR at −0.052 m. The real offset is +0.052 m.

FixCorrect the sign in the URDF.

ResultThe largest accuracy gain of the project. The 5–10 cm stop error went away.

02

Heading drifted

ProblemHeading drifted 5.6°/min while the robot was still. Gyro bias added up. The built-in IMU was even less accurate.

FixUse the Yesense IMU. Fuse its absolute yaw, not only its turn rate.

ResultDrift settled at about 1–2° and stopped growing.

03

Turns were too slow

ProblemThe motors ignore small commands (deadband). The robot turned much slower than commanded, so the controller and EKF lagged.

FixMultiply the turn command by 3 in the driver (max 3 rad/s).

ResultThe biggest performance gain: smoother turns, less lag, and a higher speed (0.30 m/s).

04

The computer was overloaded

ProblemController 20 → 1 Hz. EKF 50 → 17 Hz. TF errors.

FixLess costmap work. Replan only when the path is not valid.

ResultBack to 20 Hz and 50 Hz.

Run 3: what happened

O: 10.7 → 0.7 cm, confirmed. C: 7.0 → 1.4 cm, but the stop check timed out. The script then ended the run. A and G were not tried.

Why

We tuned on one half of the course. The other half had more wheel slip. The robot did not stay still for 1 s within the 4 s limit.

What we do next
  1. Skip the cell on a stop timeout. Do not end the run.
  2. Filter velocity noise in the stop check.
  3. Test on all parts of the course, not one half.
